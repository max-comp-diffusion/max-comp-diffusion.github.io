Mode-Averaging Problem in Compositional Diffusion

When local plan distributions are multimodal, neighboring local plans may be denoised toward incompatible modes. A local plan conditioned on these neighbors through its overlaps can then have a denoised estimate between the modes, in a low-density region. The composed plan can therefore be infeasible even when each mode represents feasible local behavior. In the circular-arc example, the local diffusion model is trained on short segments along the circle, and three overlapping local plans are composed to connect the start and goal . A trajectory is valid when all its states and segment midpoints lie within the shaded ring, so feasible plans follow either the upper or the lower half of the circle. Invalid plans are drawn in red. CompDiffuser often generates trajectories through the interior of the circle, where they depart from the regions supported by the training data, and increasing the number of denoising steps does not reliably resolve this. Using the same trained model and composition rule, MaxCD produces more valid plans along the circle.

Preserving fine details

The denoised estimate used in the maximal-noise transition approximates a posterior mean, in which uncertain fine details can be averaged out. Repeatedly applying this transition across all frequency components can therefore suppress fine details. To preserve them, a spectral split applies maximal-noise transitions to low-frequency components, which can revise coarse trajectory geometry, and retains the original transition for high-frequency components. The split is taken along the local-plan horizon in planning and along the spatial dimensions in panorama generation.

Compositional update

Neighboring local plans exchange information through their overlaps. We apply the candidate with the spectral split to the overlap coordinates of interior local plans and retain the original transition elsewhere. The start- and goal-conditioned plans keep the original transition throughout. The update is used only while t/T > γ, reserving the low-noise stages for refinement with the original transition. Both candidates reuse the same denoised estimate and noise prediction, so MaxCD requires no retraining or additional denoiser evaluations. The main OGBench success-rate and panorama experiments use a common noise-level threshold γ = 0.2 and spectral bandwidth ρ = 0.25, without tuning these hyperparameters separately for each task.

Plan Feasibility Analysis

We sample 20 plans for each of the five test-time tasks in AntMaze-Giant-Stitch and measure the valid plan rate, the fraction of plans that do not pass through maze walls, for CompDiffuser (CD), ECD, CDGS and MaxCD with the same pretrained local model and start and goal conditions. CompDiffuser produces many infeasible plans that pass through walls, while MaxCD generates more valid plans that follow the corridors. In the paper's AntMaze comparison, MaxCD raises the mean valid plan rate from 36% to 86% across the five tasks. The appendix provides the same comparison on PointMaze-Giant-Stitch.

Conclusion

MaxCD addresses persistent mode-averaging by changing the sampling transition on overlaps. The spectral split preserves the original transition on high-frequency components, with no retraining or additional denoiser evaluations. Experiments show improved plan feasibility and higher success rates on challenging locomotion and object manipulation tasks, with substantial gains on the Giant mazes. The same transition improves visual consistency and prompt alignment in panorama generation with nearly the same generation time as the base sampler.
